> Under a finite nonempty candidate bank, finite larger-is-better locking scores, nonnegative \(\delta\), nonempty feature sets, and one common canonical feature universe, the eligible pool is nonempty because every score maximizer has regret zero. Selection is restricted to this pool; therefore the locked candidate satisfies \(R_{\widehat r}\leq\delta\) for singleton and nonsingleton pools and along every deterministic tie path. At \(\delta=0\), the pool is exactly the set of score maximizers and need not be a singleton; when \(\delta\geq\max_rR_r\), the method is the full-bank Jaccard medoid. Complete statements and proofs are provided in Supplementary Methods.
>
> Candidate feature lists are encoded as `uint8` binary masks in the common ordered universe. Nonsingleton ties are resolved by larger mean eligible-pool Jaccard, larger \(L_r\), smaller \(|F_r|\), and lexicographically smaller SHA-256 mask digest. The digest is a stable ordering device, not a scientific score; collision resistance is an engineering assumption, not the source of regret feasibility. Duplicate masks from distinct runs remain multiple voting candidates, so multiplicity is part of the candidate bank. Exact duplicate records represent the same scientific feature set, and source-run ID is retained only as provenance after feature-set selection. This content-derived rule makes the selected feature set invariant to candidate input-row permutations.
>
> Straightforward dense-mask construction costs \(O(Rp)\); the current sparse-set Jaccard calculation and complete ordered pairwise audit cost expected \(O(E^2s)\) time and \(O(Rp+E^2)\) memory, with \(R\) retained candidates, \(E\) eligible candidates, universe size \(p\), and average selected-set size \(s\). For the present \(R=5\) design this is at most 10 distinct off-diagonal comparisons (25 ordered entries including the diagonal), but this small use case does not establish large-bank scalability.
>
> These propositions concern only the configured empirical development-CV score gap among supplied candidates. They do not establish expected predictive-risk improvement, unbiased generalization error, external validity, statistical certainty, participant-resampling or biomarker stability, predictive superiority, or clinical utility.
