\section{Introduction}

High-dimensional biomedical tables often contain far more predictors than participants, making fitted models and selected signatures sensitive to preprocessing decisions, finite samples, and stochastic search paths \citep{ref01,ref02,ref03}. Feature selection can make a model easier to inspect and deploy, but an apparently compact signature is not credible if held-out outcomes informed preprocessing, hyperparameters, a stochastic seed, or the reported feature set \citep{ref06,ref07,ref08}. Favorable-run selection is a particularly quiet form of leakage: several stochastic runs are generated, but only the run with the most favorable downstream evaluation is reported.

RFECV provides an auditable nested elimination path and can supply a target feature count, whereas genetic search can explore nonnested subsets but introduces run-to-run variability \citep{ref12,ref13,ref15,ref16}. A rule that simply chooses the highest development-CV run preserves performance but ignores whether the selected subset is representative of the other runs. Conversely, a medoid over an unrestricted pool may favor agreement at an unacceptably large development-score loss. The operational problem is therefore a compression--regret problem: one needs a representative stochastic output whose loss relative to the best development-scored candidate is explicit and bounded.

The archived WrapEvoFS analyses used a top-three Jaccard-medoid lock and a zero-truncated size-penalized genetic fitness. Those choices remain reproducible as legacy modes. Audit of the saved AMP-AD runs, however, identified a concrete flattening failure: in one SVM-L1/Rush/Small-cap condition the RFECV target was 3, the locked set contained 84 features, four of five run-best penalized scores were zero, and the best population fitness was zero in 245 of 250 saved generations. This failure motivated an upgraded method rather than a cosmetic reinterpretation of the legacy analysis.

WrapEvoFS now makes three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item development-only leakage safeguards that exclude held-out outcomes from preprocessing, strategy selection, tolerance selection, run selection, and locking;
\item auditable target-guided stochastic subset search with an untruncated recommended objective, deterministic ordering, flattening diagnostics, and an explicit legacy compatibility path; and
\item representative locking under a strict empirical development-CV score-gap constraint, with singleton eligibility permitted and complete machine-readable decision records.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical objective is not predictive superiority. We evaluate compression, development-CV regret, locking sensitivity, and five-seed agreement first, then treat held-out performance as secondary evaluation. Bayesian uncertainty quantification, STABL, and BLiP are retained as secondary interoperability analyses rather than evidence of biomarker validation.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Overall analytical workflow and leakage boundary}

\cref{fig:main1} separates development-only selection from one-time held-out evaluation. Starting from supplied matrices, fitted preprocessing and Direct candidate generation precede RFECV target estimation. Within each Direct branch, five target-guided genetic searches generate five retained run-best candidate subsets. Every retained candidate then undergoes fixed development-CV rescoring with the locking evaluator. If \(L_r\) is candidate \(r\)'s fixed locking score and \(L_{\mathrm{best}}=\max_r L_r\), empirical development-CV regret is \(R_r=L_{\mathrm{best}}-L_r\), and the prespecified tolerance defines the regret-eligible pool \(E_\delta=\{r:R_r\leq\delta\}\). Pairwise eligible-pool Jaccard similarities and each candidate's mean Jaccard are computed only within \(E_\delta\); the largest-mean-Jaccard candidate is the Jaccard medoid, subject to prespecified deterministic tie-breaking. The selected signature, settings, and final model-development procedure become the locked output before any held-out access. Held-out labels, predictions, and metrics are not arguments to the locking API and cannot determine the strategy, tolerance, fitness mode, penalty, metric, tie-breaking rule, or selected run. The final estimator is then fitted on all available development data and used for one-time held-out evaluation; ``without refitting'' means that held-out outcomes never trigger feature selection, hyperparameter changes, or another fit.

\subsection{Software implementation, modes, and artifacts}

WrapEvoFS v0.2.0 is the peer-review submission release and targets Python 3.10--3.12 through the \texttt{WrapEvoPipeline} Python API and \texttt{wrapevofs} command-line interface. YAML configuration controls preprocessing, Direct selection, RFECV, genetic search, locking, scoring, random seeds, checkpoint/resume behavior, and artifact output. The documented repository URL is \url{https://github.com/junseoparkX/wrapevofs-package}; the repository remains private during revision and is described as public only after an actual public release.

Two locking strategies and two genetic-fitness modes are explicit. \path{top_k_jaccard_medoid} and \path{legacy_zero_truncated_linear} preserve archived behavior. New analyses may use \path{regret_constrained_medoid} and the recommended \path{untruncated_shifted_linear} mode. Historical configurations that omit the new fields retain the legacy defaults; archived numerical results are labeled legacy and are not silently replaced.

Artifact schema v2.1 exports run identifiers, feature counts, locking scores and fold scores where available, score SD and SE, absolute and relative regret, strict eligibility status, pairwise and mean Jaccard values, selection flags, tie-breaking paths, metrics, seeds, software version, and configuration, input, and candidate-universe fingerprints. GA histories separately retain raw objective, legacy-truncated fitness, sampling weight, feature count, target deviation, penalty amount, zero-fitness fraction, objective diversity, uniform parent-sampling fallback, population-mask diversity, and warnings. The lock audit records that no candidate was added outside the declared tolerance. Checkpoint continuation uses an atomically replaced, pickle-free state and rejects package, schema, configuration, input, feature-order, population, or backend mismatches rather than silently restarting.

\subsection{RFECV target and primary estimands}

For branch (m), Direct denotes the branch-specific pre-GA screening procedure (SVM-L1, XGBoost, or Boruta-RF), and its candidate set is \(\mathcal C_m\) with size \(p_m\). RFECV evaluates its nested elimination path using development folds and chooses the smallest count attaining the largest mean score among counts allowed by the configured cap:

\begin{equation}
k_m^{\ast}=\min\!\arg\max_{k\in\mathcal K_m^{\mathrm{elig}}}\overline S_{R,m}(k).
\label{eq:rfecv-target}
\end{equation}

For a selected set \(F\), the compression ratio relative to the Direct set is

\begin{equation}
C(F)=1-\frac{|F|}{p_m}.
\label{eq:compression}
\end{equation}

All locking metrics are oriented so that larger values are better; the demonstrated API supports classification accuracy, balanced accuracy, binary AUROC, and multiclass one-vs-rest AUROC, not regression losses. Each Direct branch contributes five retained run-best candidates to fixed development-CV rescoring. For retained run-best candidate (r), let \(L_r\) be its fixed development-CV locking score and \(L_{\mathrm{best}}=\max_r L_r\). The empirical development-CV score gap, termed empirical development-CV regret, is

\begin{equation}
R_r=L_{\mathrm{best}}-L_r.
\label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}

Run-to-run agreement is summarized by mean pairwise Jaccard, selected-candidate mean Jaccard, and the chance-corrected Nogueira coefficient only when a complete run-by-feature matrix exists \citep{ref36}. Because each condition contains five stochastic seeds on one fixed development sample, these quantities are called seed agreement, run-to-run agreement, or stochastic agreement. They are not participant-resampling stability or biomarker stability. Held-out performance delta is computed only after a rule is fixed and is secondary:

\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}}=M(F^{\mathrm{lock}})-M(\mathcal C_m).
\label{eq:heldout-delta}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Recommended and legacy genetic objectives}

A nonempty binary chromosome \(\mathbf z\in\{0,1\}^{p_m}\) represents feature set \(F_m(\mathbf z)\). Let \(\overline S_{G,m}(\mathbf z)\) be its mean development-fold base score and \(\lambda\) the penalty per feature of deviation from \(k_m^{\ast}\). Both modes compute

\begin{equation}
Q_m(\mathbf z)=\overline S_{G,m}(\mathbf z)-\lambda\left||F_m(\mathbf z)|-k_m^{\ast}\right|.
\label{eq:raw-objective}
\end{equation}

The legacy mode ranks chromosomes by

\begin{equation}
\Phi_m^{\mathrm{legacy}}(\mathbf z)=\max\{0,Q_m(\mathbf z)\}.
\label{eq:legacy-fitness}
\end{equation}

The recommended untruncated mode uses \(Q_m\) directly for scientific reporting, ranking, elite selection, and run-best updating. For proportional parent sampling only, nonnegative weights are obtained by a shift that cannot change objective ordering:

\begin{equation}
w_i=Q_i-\min_{j:Q_j\ \mathrm{finite}}Q_j+\epsilon.
\label{eq:shifted-weight}
\end{equation}

Here \(\epsilon=10^{-12}\). Uniform sampling is recorded when no objective is finite, the objective range is at most \(\epsilon\), the weight total is nonfinite, or the total is at most \(\epsilon\). Untruncated ties are resolved by higher raw objective, higher base score, smaller target deviation, smaller feature count, and a SHA-256 hash of the binary chromosome bytes; this hash is independent of Python's process-randomized hash. Empty chromosomes remain prohibited. Warnings identify more than 50\% zero legacy fitness, all-zero legacy populations, uniform fallback, excessive target deviation, and loss of mask diversity.

\subsection{Development-only AMP-AD Rush recommended-mode sensitivity}

A limited prospective recommended-mode sensitivity reran six AMP-AD Rush branch--cap conditions: SVM-L1, XGBoost, and Boruta-RF at the Small and Reference caps, with five seeds per condition (30 full GPU GA runs). All searches used \(\lambda=0.015\) and the untruncated raw objective in \cref{eq:raw-objective} for ranking, elitism, and run-best updating; shifted nonnegative weights were used only for parent sampling. The matched archived runs provide the legacy zero-truncated comparison. Recommended run-best masks were rescored by the fixed five-fold development-CV macro one-vs-rest AUROC evaluator and locked with absolute regret tolerance \(\delta=0.01\). Strict eligible-only pools permitted a singleton, added no candidate outside the tolerance, and used the prespecified deterministic medoid tie-breaking path. The comparison was specified as a mechanism and target-fidelity sensitivity analysis. Rush held-out labels, predictions, and outcomes were not loaded, accessed, or evaluated, and no held-out performance claim was tested.

\subsection{Regret-constrained medoid locking}

For absolute tolerance \(\delta\), candidate (r) is eligible when \(R_r\le\delta\). Relative regret is

\begin{equation}
R_r^{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac{R_r}{\max(|L_{\mathrm{best}}|,\epsilon)},
\label{eq:relative-regret}
\end{equation}

and relative eligibility requires \(R_r^{\mathrm{rel}}\le\delta\). A secondary best-run-SE-scaled tolerance defines eligibility by \(R_r\le\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{best}}\), where the SE is computed only from the best run's development-fold locking scores. This is not the conventional paired one-standard-error model-selection rule because it does not estimate the uncertainty of the paired score difference. If the best run's fold scores are absent, this sensitivity stops with an actionable error; means and SDs are not reverse-engineered into fold vectors.

The regret-eligible pool is \(E_\delta=\{r:R_r\leq\delta\}\) for absolute tolerance and is never expanded with candidates whose regret exceeds the declared threshold. Because the best-scoring candidate has zero regret, \(E_\delta\) is nonempty for every nonnegative tolerance. If \(|E_\delta|=1\), its sole candidate is selected. If \(|E_\delta|>1\), pairwise eligible-pool Jaccard similarity and mean Jaccard are computed within \(E_\delta\):

\begin{equation}
\overline J_r=\frac{1}{|E_\delta|-1}\sum_{\substack{s\in E_\delta\\s\ne r}}
\frac{|F_r\cap F_s|}{|F_r\cup F_s|}.
\label{eq:eligible-medoid}
\end{equation}

The candidate maximizing \(\overline J_r\) is the Jaccard medoid of the regret-eligible pool. For nonsingleton pools, ties are resolved by higher \(\overline J_r\), higher \(L_r\), smaller \(|F_r|\), and then the lexicographically smaller stable SHA-256 hash of the canonical binary feature mask. The hash is a deterministic ordering device, not a scientific score. Exact duplicate masks are retained as multiple voting candidates; if duplicate records share the winning scientific key, source-run ID is used only to retain a provenance representative after the feature set has been selected, never to choose between distinct masks.

For every finite nonempty candidate bank, finite larger-is-better scores, common canonical feature universe, nonempty feature sets, and \(\delta\geq0\), the eligible pool is nonempty because a score maximizer has zero regret. Selection is restricted to that pool, so the locked candidate satisfies \(R_{\widehat r}\leq\delta\) under every singleton, medoid, and deterministic tie path. At \(\delta=0\), all and only highest-scoring candidates are eligible: a unique maximizer is selected directly, whereas tied maximizers undergo medoid selection. If \(\delta\geq\max_rR_r\), the rule is the full-bank medoid. Complete statements and proofs are given in the Supplementary Methods.

With \(R\) retained candidates, \(E\) eligible candidates, canonical-universe size \(p\), and average selected-set size \(s\), the current dense-mask, sparse-Jaccard, complete-audit implementation has expected time \(O(Rp+E^2s+R\log R)\) and memory \(O(Rp+E^2)\). For the present \(R=5\) design this means at most 10 unordered eligible pairs (25 ordered matrix entries including the diagonal), but the five-run experiments do not establish large-bank scalability.

The selected candidate therefore always satisfies the configured empirical development-CV score-gap threshold and becomes the locked output before held-out evaluation. This is only a feasibility and representative-selection guarantee within the supplied candidate bank; empirical regret is not expected predictive risk, generalization error, or statistical uncertainty, and the result does not establish predictive superiority, external validity, biomarker stability, unbiased generalization performance, or globally optimal feature selection. The recommended operating tolerance for this revision is absolute \(\delta=0.01\); it is treated as a metric-scale practical margin and was not optimized using held-out results. Development-only sensitivity evaluates 0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, and the best-run-SE-scaled threshold where fold vectors exist.

\subsection{Empirical datasets and analysis boundary}

The analysis uses three existing fixed designs summarized in \cref{tab:main1}. The ADNI-derived multiclass analysis contains 532 development and 133 held-out participants with 7,411 processed predictors \citep{ref20,ref21}. The AMP-AD analysis contains 527 participants and 13,136 supplied frontal-proteomic and metabolomic predictors in four fixed leave-one-center-out evaluations. The CGGA MGMT-methylation analysis contains 214 development and 92 held-out samples with 24,326 aligned gene-expression predictors \citep{ref23}. These are internal demonstrations beginning from supplied matrices; upstream assay processing was not re-audited and external validation was not performed.

\input{tables/table_02.tex}

The retrospective development-only reanalysis used all available five-run masks and saved locking scores. CGGA candidate fold scores were recomputed with the archived five-fold ROC-AUROC evaluator, seeds, and fixed random-forest settings, enabling best-run-SE-scaled sensitivity. AMP-AD retained all five masks and development-CV means but not fold vectors, so that sensitivity was unavailable. ADNI retained candidate scores and the locked mask but not all five masks or fold vectors, so all-run and regret-constrained medoids could not be reconstructed; those cells are explicitly marked unavailable rather than imputed.

The supplied matrices were split before model development, but preprocessing and label-dependent Direct screening were fitted once on the full development partition rather than refitted inside every locking-score fold. RFECV, GA evaluation, and locking then reused configured development folds during adaptive model and subset search; the locking evaluator used a fixed stratified five-fold construction with random state 42. Candidate evaluators used run-derived estimator seeds, so five-run agreement can reflect both search and estimator randomness. Accordingly, \(L_r\) and \(R_r\) are operational post-selection development scores and score gaps, not unbiased estimates of out-of-sample generalization error. The untouched held-out partitions provide the separate post-lock evaluation boundary.

\subsection{Stage-metric audit}

Each configuration records the RFECV metric, GA base-score metric, locking metric, and held-out metrics. All archived configurations use mixed development objectives: ADNI and AMP-AD combine weighted multiclass AUROC, balanced accuracy, and a locking metric; CGGA combines RFECV AUROC, GA accuracy, and locking AUROC. These legacy workflows are therefore staged heuristics, not unified optimizers. A future \texttt{scoring.unified\_metric: macro\_ovr\_auroc} option maps to binary ROC AUROC or multiclass macro OvR AUROC when compatible; it does not retroactively change archived runs.

\subsection{Secondary held-out and interoperability analyses}

Held-out results were joined only after each development-only rule was fixed. A held-out estimate for a newly selected rule was reused only when its selected run exactly matched an archived evaluated run; otherwise it remains unavailable. No held-out value was used to select \(\delta\) or compare locking strategies. Existing paired intervals remain identified as paired; point-only differences are not presented as intervals.

Bayesian uncertainty quantification, training-only STABL, and BLiP remain secondary interoperability analyses \citep{ref25,ref30,ref32,ref33,ref34}. They show that development-derived locked outputs can define a downstream candidate set for posterior inference, resampling-oriented selection, and posterior expected-FDR decisions. Detailed priors, coefficient tables, trace diagnostics, complete stability paths, posterior summaries, and BLiP sensitivities remain in the Supplementary Information. Cross-method overlap is not interpreted as biomarker validation.

\section{Results}

Results are identified as (A) legacy end-to-end demonstrations, which reproduce the archived zero-truncated objective and top-three lock; (B) retrospective strict re-locking, which applies the new lock to archived candidate banks without regenerating the GA candidates; or (C) prospective recommended-mode analyses, which require newly executed untruncated searches. The results below contain categories A and B and one deliberately limited category-C analysis: a development-only AMP-AD Rush objective sensitivity. Category C is not inferred from retrospective re-locking and does not include held-out Rush evaluation.

\subsection{Retrospective strict re-locking: compression and empirical score gap}

Across 36 AMP-AD branch-by-center-by-cap conditions, retrospective strict re-locking reduced Direct feature counts by a median 74.1\% (range 15.8\%--80.0\%). Median empirical development-CV score gap was 0 and the maximum was 0.0091, remaining within the declared 0.01 threshold. Across six CGGA branch-by-guidance conditions, median compression was 57.9\% (range 34.3\%--70.0\%), median gap was 0.00065, and maximum gap was 0.00355. These calculations validate the strict lock on legacy-generated candidate banks; they do not validate the recommended untruncated search. The upgraded ADNI calculation was unavailable because all five candidate masks were not archived. Its three legacy top-three medoids compressed by a median 54.0\% and had median development-score gap 0.0140, which illustrates why the top-three rule does not itself impose a score-loss bound (\cref{fig:supp15}; \cref{tab:main2}).

\subsection{Retrospective strict-lock sensitivity}

Increasing absolute tolerance enlarged the strict eligible pool in both datasets (\cref{fig:supp16}). At \(\delta=0\), every condition had a singleton pool containing a highest-scoring candidate and selected gap was 0. At \(\delta=0.01\), mean pool size was 2.64 in AMP-AD and 2.50 in CGGA; median selected gaps were 0 and 0.00065, respectively. At \(\delta=0.02\), mean pools expanded to 4.19 and 3.83 and median selected gaps increased to 0.00328 and 0.00242. Mean selected Jaccard changed only modestly over this range and was not monotone. CGGA best-run-SE-scaled pools had mean size 4.17 and median selected gap 0.00372; AMP-AD sensitivity was not computed because fold vectors were absent. All 168 available absolute-tolerance selections satisfied their declared threshold and no fallback candidate was added. These development-only results support 0.01 as a practical operating point, not as a held-out-optimized or uniquely optimal constant.

\subsection{Retrospective candidate-bank seed agreement}

Complete five-mask matrices supported mean pairwise Jaccard, selected-candidate mean Jaccard, and Nogueira agreement for AMP-AD and CGGA. Under retrospective strict re-locking, median selected mean Jaccard was 0.204 in AMP-AD and 0.424 in CGGA. Median Nogueira coefficients were negative in AMP-AD and 0.076 in CGGA, indicating limited agreement relative to the chance correction in these small five-run samples. These are noisy seed-agreement summaries on fixed development sets. Locking makes run choice deterministic after candidates exist; it does not make the selected features stable under participant resampling or establish biomarkers.

\subsection{Legacy diagnostic: penalty flattening}

Eighteen of 36 AMP-AD configurations triggered at least one legacy flattening warning; none of the six CGGA configurations did. The most severe condition was AMP-AD SVM-L1/Rush/Small-cap: target \(k^*=3\), locked count 84, target deviation 81, four of five retained run-best legacy fitness values equal to zero, and 245 all-zero generations. Under the archived roulette implementation, a zero best fitness implies an all-zero population and therefore uniform parent-sampling fallback, giving 245 inferred fallbacks. This is a demonstrated failure mode of the zero-truncated objective, not merely a useful stress test (\cref{fig:supp17}; \cref{tab:main3}). The untruncated mode removes the truncation, preserves ordering among negative objectives, and records sampling shifts separately; it does not retroactively alter the archived results.

\subsection{Prospective recommended-mode objective sensitivity in AMP-AD Rush}

The six-condition development-only rerun provided direct mechanism evidence for the recommended untruncated search without repeating the full AMP-AD study (\cref{fig:supp18,tab:supp16}). Aggregate absolute target deviation decreased from 110 to 48 (mean 18.33 to 8.00); fidelity improved in four conditions, was unchanged in one, and differed by one feature in the remaining condition. Diagnostic zero legacy-truncated run-best values decreased from 4/30 to 0/30, and the corresponding all-zero-generation diagnostic decreased from 362 to 133. Actual shifted-weight searches recorded no uniform parent-sampling fallback. The most severe SVM-L1/Rush/Small-cap result moved from a legacy locked count of 84 to 28 for target \(k^*=3\), a substantial correction although not exact target attainment.

Fixed development-CV locking scores were closely preserved overall: the recommended-minus-legacy difference averaged \(-0.00114\) and ranged from \(-0.00996\) to 0.01004 across the six conditions. Selected mean Jaccard was higher in two nonsingleton conditions and lower in two; the two strict singleton pools have no defined within-pool mean Jaccard and are reported as NA. Thus, the prospective evidence supports reduced objective flattening and improved aggregate target fidelity, rather than universal agreement gains. All 30 runs completed in approximately 12.61 GPU-hours in aggregate. Held-out Rush outcomes were not accessed or evaluated.

The ADNI-derived locked outputs supported a secondary 81-candidate restricted Bayesian analysis, a training-only STABL analysis, and singleton-group BLiP decisions. The restricted Bayesian analysis provided posterior uncertainty and sampling diagnostics; STABL selected eight development-derived features; and BLiP selection counts changed with the expected-FDR and practical-effect settings. Their role here is interoperability: a locked, auditable feature set can be handed to complementary uncertainty and selection frameworks without reopening held-out-informed candidate choice.

\section{Discussion}

The most consistent empirical result was compression. Retrospective strict re-locking reduced AMP-AD and CGGA Direct candidate sets substantially while keeping the observed adaptive internal development-score gap within 0.01. Predictive changes were less consistent: available held-out effects varied in direction, many intervals crossed zero, and upgraded selections without archived evaluations were left missing. This evidence supports auditable compression and standardized run selection within legacy-generated candidate banks, not predictive superiority, equivalence, non-inferiority, or prospective validation of the recommended search.

The regret constraint changes the scientific meaning of representative locking. The legacy top-three rule first imposed an arbitrary rank cutoff and then chose a medoid; it could return a candidate with a score gap larger than an investigator considered acceptable. The upgraded procedure uses fixed development-CV rescoring to define an interpretable empirical development-CV regret budget and its regret-eligible pool before using eligible-pool Jaccard agreement to choose the Jaccard medoid. Singleton regret-eligible pools are valid and no candidate outside the threshold can enter the selection argmax. Canonical stable-hash tie-breaking and complete audit rows make the selected feature set reproducible without using input-row order or source-run ID as a scientific tie-break. Formally, this guarantees only the configured empirical development-CV score gap within the retained candidate bank; it does not establish predictive superiority, external validity, biomarker stability, unbiased generalization performance, or globally optimal feature selection.

The untruncated objective addresses a separate failure mechanism. In the AMP-AD SVM-L1/Rush/Small-cap condition, zero truncation erased objective differences across nearly the entire optimization history while the selected count remained far from the RFECV target. Reporting that condition only as a stress test would understate the methodological problem. The recommended mode keeps negative raw objectives ordered and shifts them only for sampling. Legacy behavior remains necessary for reproduction, but it is not the recommended mode for new analyses.

The new AMP-AD Rush sensitivity strengthens this mechanism argument prospectively. Across 30 newly executed development-only searches, the recommended objective eliminated zero run-best values under the legacy-truncation diagnostic, reduced all-zero-generation diagnostics, and improved aggregate target fidelity while keeping fixed development-CV locking-score differences small and mixed. The remaining target mismatch in SVM-L1/Small-cap shows that \(k^*\) is a soft search target rather than a cardinality constraint. These results establish that the recommended objective behaves more informatively in the tested search setting; they are not evidence of improved held-out performance or a universal increase in run-to-run agreement.

Five saved seeds provide limited evidence about stochastic agreement. Mean Jaccard and Nogueira summaries can reveal dispersion across run-best masks, but they do not estimate participant-resampling stability, biological reproducibility, or uniqueness of an optimum. A medoid standardizes which candidate is handed downstream; it does not turn a seed-dependent set into a stable biomarker signature. More seeds and explicit participant-level resampling would be required for stronger stability claims.

Metric alignment also matters. The archived RFECV, GA, and locking stages optimize different development metrics, so the legacy workflow is a staged heuristic. The new unified-metric option supports future compatible analyses, but it does not imply that RFECV's nested path and a GA's nonnested search optimize the same mathematical problem. Each stage and its metric should remain visible in the audit record.

Bayesian, STABL, and BLiP add useful but secondary views. Bayesian models quantify uncertainty conditional on a development-derived candidate set; STABL emphasizes recurrence under its resampling scheme; BLiP converts posterior quantities into expected-FDR-constrained decisions. Agreement among them is not independent replication because all reuse the same development data and screening boundary. Their value is to demonstrate reproducible downstream hand-off and to clarify how different objectives alter selected sets.

The study has important boundaries. Evaluations use fixed partitions or four fixed held-out centers; preprocessing and Direct screening were fitted on each full development partition rather than nested within the locking-score folds; repeated fold reuse makes locking scores adaptive internal selection scores; tolerance sensitivity is internal to development data; and five seeds are sparse. Candidate evaluators used run-derived seeds, so observed seed agreement can combine search and estimator randomness. The verified analysis starts from supplied matrices rather than raw-assay processing. Archived AMP-AD fold vectors and ADNI candidate masks were not fully available, preventing best-run-SE-scaled sensitivity or upgraded locking in those legacy settings. These limitations are stated without treating missing analyses as negative results. External validation, end-to-end recommended-mode evaluation with prespecified held-out assessment, more extensive seed and participant resampling, and raw-data provenance audits remain necessary before biological or clinical interpretation.

\section{Conclusion}

WrapEvoFS provides a development-only, auditable rule that uses fixed development-CV rescoring, a regret-eligible pool, and its Jaccard medoid to select one representative subset from a stochastic candidate bank under a strict empirical development-CV regret constraint. It preserves legacy reproducibility while recommending an untruncated genetic objective, records failure diagnostics, and permits singleton regret-eligible pools rather than relaxing the declared threshold. The selected signature becomes the locked output before one-time held-out evaluation. The strongest demonstrated outcomes are retrospective compression of complete archived AMP-AD and CGGA candidate banks with observed gaps below 0.01 and, in a limited prospective development-only AMP-AD Rush sensitivity, improved aggregate target fidelity with reduced zero-truncation diagnostics and no uniform-sampling fallback. The Rush sensitivity did not access held-out outcomes; predictive superiority, unique optimality, and biomarker confirmation are not claimed.
