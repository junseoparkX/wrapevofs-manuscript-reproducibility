\begin{abstract}
High-dimensional feature-selection workflows are vulnerable to held-out leakage and favorable-run selection when several stochastic searches yield plausible signatures. WrapEvoFS confines preprocessing, branch-specific Direct pre-GA candidate generation, RFECV target estimation, repeated genetic search, fixed development-CV rescoring, and representative locking to development data. In the upgraded workflow, five retained run-best candidates are re-scored with the fixed development-CV locking evaluator; a prespecified empirical development-CV regret tolerance defines the regret-eligible pool, and its Jaccard medoid is selected using deterministic tie-breaking without relaxing the tolerance. The resulting locked output is fixed before one-time held-out evaluation. An untruncated shifted-linear genetic objective is recommended because it preserves rank information when penalized objectives are negative; the archived zero-truncated objective remains available for exact legacy reproduction. Retrospective strict re-locking of complete archived AMP-AD and CGGA candidate banks showed that compression was the most consistent empirical finding, whereas held-out changes were mixed and uncertain. In a limited 30-run development-only AMP-AD Rush sensitivity, the recommended objective reduced aggregate absolute target deviation from 110 to 48 and the zero-truncation all-zero-generation diagnostic from 362 to 133, with no uniform-sampling fallbacks; held-out Rush outcomes were not accessed. Agreement across five seeds is reported as stochastic or seed agreement, not participant-resampling stability or biomarker stability. Bayesian uncertainty quantification, STABL, and BLiP are retained as secondary interoperability analyses demonstrating downstream use of development-derived locked outputs. WrapEvoFS therefore provides an auditable development-only rule for stochastic feature compression under a strict empirical score-gap constraint and a reproducible hand-off to downstream analysis.
\keywords{feature selection; stochastic compression; development-CV regret; genetic algorithm; representative locking; reproducible software}
\end{abstract}
